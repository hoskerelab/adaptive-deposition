Material behavior changes during printing, while geometric tolerances remain strict. A discrete element method (DEM) model provides training data for the initial control policy. During execution, laser measurements update a learned deposition model in real time, allowing the robot to adjust speed and nozzle offset. A related system applies online feedback to robotic crack filling.

DISCRETE ELEMENT METHOD

Particle-based deposition model

DEM represents mortar as interacting particles governed by Hertz–Mindlin contact mechanics and JKR adhesion. Varying cohesion, nozzle speed, and offset produces data for offline reinforcement learning.

The DEM model uses simplified material parameters. During physical printing, the controller updates its learned geometry predictor; DEM parameters remain fixed.

COMPACT FORMULATIONDEM / RL / IELM / MPC
DEM
\[ m_i \ddot{\mathbf{x}}_i = \sum_j \left(\mathbf{f}_{ij}^{n}+\mathbf{f}_{ij}^{t}+\mathbf{f}_{ij}^{adh}\right) + m_i\mathbf{g} \]

Particle forces determine the simulated material motion.

RL prior
\[ u_k^{\mathrm{RL}} = \pi_\theta(s_k), \qquad \theta^\star = \arg\max_\theta \mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_k r_k\right] \]

Offline RL learns a policy from simulated deposition data.

Online IELM
\[ \hat{y}_k = H(u_k)\beta_k, \qquad \beta_k = \beta_{k-1} + K_k\!\left(y_k - H(u_k)\beta_{k-1}\right) \]

Recursive updates fit the geometry predictor to laser measurements.

Constrained control
\[ u_k^\star = \arg\min_{u\in\mathcal{U}} \lVert \hat{y}_{k+1}(u)-y^\star \rVert_Q^2 + \lVert u-u_k^{\mathrm{RL}} \rVert_R^2 \]

MPC selects nozzle commands within predefined limits.

Here, \(u_k=(v_k,o_k)\) denotes nozzle speed and vertical offset, and \(y_k=(w_k,h_k)\) denotes the measured layer width and height.

SOURCE: ARSLAN, MANGI & HOSKERE, JCCE; ARSLAN DISSERTATION, CH. 4
